Detect forwarder cycles by assembly identity, not depth alone

Metadata is untrusted input, and docs/design/bounded-metadata-traversal.md
requires that cycles be "detected by identity, not merely stopped by a depth
ceiling". The forwarder hop added for #3400 was bounded only by a hop ceiling,
so an A -> B -> A chain rescanned both assemblies to the ceiling instead of
stopping on the repeat visit.

Thread a visited-identity set through TryResolveTopLevelTypeInAssembly, seeded
with the assembly the TypeRef already named so a forwarder pointing back at the
facade counts as a repeat visit. Keep MaxTypeForwarderHops as a second bound on
chain length.

Gate termination with a synthetic two-assembly forwarder cycle. No compiler
emits a forwarder cycle, so hand-built metadata is the only way to reach the
state. Tamper-verified: removing both bounds crashes the test process with
STATUS_STACK_OVERFLOW (0xC00000FD).

The narrower identity-vs-depth property -- that each assembly is rescanned at
most once -- is reasoned but not gated, and the test says so: the resolved
assembly cache makes rescans invisible to any observer outside the walk, so
resolver call counts are identical under both bounds.
